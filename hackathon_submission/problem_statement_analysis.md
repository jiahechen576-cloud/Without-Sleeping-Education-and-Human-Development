# ReadRabbit — Problem Statement Analysis

## 1. Background

Reading is an important part of education, communication, and independent learning. However, not every learner interacts with written information in the same way. People with dyslexia and other reading difficulties may benefit from additional support when decoding text, following lines, understanding unfamiliar vocabulary, or expressing their ideas in writing.

Digital technology offers opportunities to make reading experiences more flexible. Yet learners may need to switch between separate applications for text-to-speech, vocabulary lookup, text recognition, speech input, and reading comprehension practice.

ReadRabbit addresses this challenge by exploring an integrated, accessible reading companion that brings several reading and learning support tools together.

## 2. Problem Statement

Learners who experience reading difficulties may find conventional digital reading experiences challenging when the presentation of text, reading assistance, and vocabulary support cannot be adapted to their needs.

Common barriers that the project aims to address include:

- **Text accessibility:** Fixed font sizes, spacing, and contrast may not suit every reader.
- **Reading guidance:** Some readers benefit from visual aids that help them keep track of the current line or word.
- **Understanding unfamiliar content:** Difficult words or complex explanations can interrupt reading and comprehension.
- **Access to printed text:** Text in images or printed materials may be difficult to use with digital reading tools without OCR.
- **Alternative input methods:** Typing can be a barrier for some learners who would benefit from speech-to-text.
- **Fragmented learning tools:** Switching between multiple applications can interrupt the reading process.

These challenges vary between individuals. ReadRabbit is designed to offer flexible support rather than assume that every person with dyslexia has the same needs.

## 3. Target Users

The primary target users are:

1. People with dyslexia who may benefit from customizable digital reading support.
2. Students who need help reading, understanding, and reviewing written material.
3. Learners who prefer listening while following text visually.
4. Users who want help understanding unfamiliar words and concepts.
5. Bahasa Melayu and English learners who benefit from language assistance.

Teachers, caregivers, and learning-support professionals may also find the prototype useful as a demonstration of accessible digital learning tools. Their needs and feedback should be explored through future user testing.

## 4. Why the Problem Matters

When reading tools do not accommodate different preferences, learners may need to spend additional effort navigating text, looking up vocabulary, or moving between applications. This can interrupt engagement with the material.

An adaptable reading experience can offer users more ways to interact with content: seeing text with personalized presentation settings, listening to speech, scanning text from an image, asking questions, and saving vocabulary for later review.

ReadRabbit explores how these capabilities can be combined in one application to support more flexible and independent learning.

## 5. Existing Gap Addressed by the Project

ReadRabbit does not claim that existing reading applications lack accessibility features. Instead, the project focuses on integrating several useful capabilities into one prototype:

- Customizable text presentation and a reading ruler.
- Text-to-speech with word highlighting.
- AI-assisted story creation and comprehension questions.
- OCR for extracting text from images.
- Speech-to-text input.
- Vocabulary saving and review.
- An AI assistant that supports Bahasa Melayu and English interactions.

The intended improvement is a more unified reading and learning workflow, reducing the need to move between separate tools for these tasks.

## 6. Problem Analysis Summary

The problem addressed by ReadRabbit is the need for a more flexible and integrated digital reading experience for learners with diverse reading needs.

The prototype responds to this need by combining accessible reading controls, speech technologies, text recognition, vocabulary support, and AI-assisted explanations. Its usefulness and effectiveness should be evaluated with representative users, including people with dyslexia, through structured testing and feedback.

## 7. Expected Benefits

ReadRabbit aims to:

- Give readers more control over text presentation.
- Support following and listening to written content.
- Make unfamiliar vocabulary easier to explore.
- Provide additional ways to access text and enter ideas.
- Encourage reading practice and comprehension review.
- Offer language support for Bahasa Melayu and English.

These are intended benefits, not proven outcomes. Further usability testing and evaluation are needed to measure their impact.

## 8. Conclusion

ReadRabbit explores how mobile technology and AI-assisted learning can make reading support more accessible and convenient. By integrating reading customization, speech, OCR, vocabulary tools, and a bilingual assistant, the prototype aims to provide learners with more flexible ways to engage with written information.

The next stage is to validate the design with users, gather accessibility feedback, and improve the application based on observed needs.
